PicoTerm

A terminal program for the TS-2068 and the TS-Pico v3 card. The card's WiFi modem runs Zimodem, and PicoTerm uses it to reach telnet BBSes and other hosts on the internet: ANSI color and box drawing, 32 or 64 columns (80 with PicoVideo), a phonebook, and XMODEM downloads to the SD card.

01What you need

  • A TS-2068 with the TS-Pico ROM 2.3 or later.
  • The TS-Pico v3 card, with firmware that has the TS2050 modem emulation (the 8251 at ports $73 and $77), and Zimodem on the card's ESP32. PicoTerm talks to Zimodem with Hayes AT commands, so the card's modem has to be in Hayes mode. PicoTerm switches it there itself where the firmware allows (section 12).
  • A WiFi network the card can join. PicoTerm can scan for one and join it (section 9).
  • An SD card in the TS-Pico, for picoterm.cfg (your settings and phonebook) and for downloads.
  • Optional: PicoVideo, for 80 columns and for color in 64 columns, on its VGA output.
A real TS2050?PicoTerm is written for the TS-Pico's modem. It doesn't pulse-dial, and it doesn't watch the TS2050's carrier line, so it won't drive an original 300-baud TS2050. Use MTERM for that.

02Loading PicoTerm

PicoTerm is one file, picoterm.tap: a one-line BASIC loader and about 15 KB of machine code, which loads at 34816 (8800h).

From the TS-Pico's SD card

  1. Copy picoterm.tap to the card's /TAP folder.
  2. Type LOAD "tpi:picoterm.tap" and press ENTER. This mounts the tape.
  3. Type LOAD "" and press ENTER. The loader runs by itself.

What happens at start-up

  1. PicoTerm reads picoterm.cfg from the card's current folder, if there is one, and sets up the screen in the column mode it says.
  2. It puts the card's modem in Hayes mode (tpi:modem hayes).
  3. It resets the 8251, which starts the card's ESP32.
  4. It sends AT once a second until Zimodem answers OK, for up to about 12 seconds: Zimodem takes a few seconds to start, longer while it joins WiFi, and you may see its banner first. Then it sends your init string (ATE1V1X1Q0 unless you've changed it) and the telnet terminal type (AT&S"41=ANSI").
  5. It turns on flow control: ATF1 for Zimodem, then tpi:modem flow on for the card. Firmware without it refuses, and PicoTerm carries on without (see the note in section 3).
  6. It shows Modem ready.

You see everything the modem says, as it says it. If something is missing, a message says so and PicoTerm carries on: section 12 explains each one.

Hayes mode staysThe TS-Pico saves the modem's mode in its config.ini, so the card stays in Hayes mode after you quit PicoTerm. MTERM and other programs written for the TS2050 need the card's 2050 mode: type SAVE "tpi:modem 2050" before you load one.
MemoryThe loader does CLEAR 34815. PicoTerm also opens the second display file with the ROM's Video Mode Change Service, the way OS-64 does, which moves BASIC up to 7B00h. It stays open after you quit, so BASIC has about 7 KB less room than usual until you reset.

03Your first call

Once the modem is ready, everything you type goes to it. Zimodem echoes your typing and answers in words (OK, CONNECT, NO CARRIER), just like a Hayes modem.

  1. Join your WiFi, once: press BREAK, then W (section 9). Zimodem remembers it if you save it.
  2. Dial a host by typing its address in quotes:
    ATDTX"bbs.example.com:23" ENTER
    The T asks Zimodem to speak telnet, which most BBSes want; the X turns on flow control for the call. Use ATDX"…" for a raw TCP port.
  3. Zimodem answers CONNECT and you're online. From now on, everything you type goes to the BBS.
  4. To hang up, press BREAK, then H. If the BBS hangs up first, you see NO CARRIER.

Typing addresses gets old quickly. Put the ones you use in the phonebook (section 8), and dial them with one key. The phonebook adds the X for you.

Why the XWhen the BBS sends faster than the 2068 can draw, the card tells Zimodem to pause (XOFF) and to go on (XON). Zimodem only obeys on a call dialed with X; on a call without it, a fast host can overrun the card and text goes missing. Flow control needs TS-Pico firmware with tpi:modem flow; with older firmware, keep MODEM_BAUD low enough for your screen (section 12).

04The keyboard

PicoTerm reads the keyboard itself. Letters, digits and SPACE type as you'd expect, and CAPS SHIFT gives capitals. Every other ASCII character and control key is on a shifted key, mostly where the 2068's own legends suggest. A held key repeats after about half a second.

Control keys

Press CAPS SHIFT and SYMBOL SHIFT together, let go, then press a letter: PicoTerm sends that letter as a control key. CAPS+SYM, then C, sends Ctrl-C (03h). CAPS+SYM twice cancels it. It works for the letters and for [ \ ] ^ _. On a call with flow control, Zimodem keeps Ctrl-S and Ctrl-Q for itself, so they don't reach the host.

05The terminal

PicoTerm has three emulations. It uses ANSI unless you choose otherwise in Settings or in a phonebook entry. At start-up and before each phonebook call, it tells Zimodem which one to report to a telnet host (ANSI, VT52 or DUMB).

EmulationForWhat it understands
ANSIBBSes: Synchronet, Mystic and the like
  • A VT100 subset with color, as BBS software expects of "ANSI-BBS".
  • Cursor: moves, positioning, save and restore.
  • Editing: erasing the screen and lines; inserting and deleting lines and characters.
  • Scrolling: a scrolling region.
  • Colors: the 8 colors with bold, blink and reverse, plus the bright colors.
  • Replies: it answers "where is the cursor?", which many BBSes use to find out the screen size, and it identifies itself as a VT100.
VT52Older systems and some Unix hostsThe standard VT52 set: cursor moves, home, direct addressing (ESC Y), erase to the end of the line and screen, reverse line feed, and identify.
TTYPlain textPrintable characters and the control codes only; escape sequences show as text.

In every emulation PicoTerm handles BEL, a short 1 kHz tone on the AY sound chip; BS; Tab, stops every 8 columns; LF; CR; and FF, which clears the screen. Text wraps at the right edge as on a VT100. A character typed in the last column leaves the cursor there, and the next one starts a new line.

Color

ANSI's eight colors become the 2068's. Bold becomes BRIGHT, blink becomes FLASH (you can turn that off in Settings), and reverse swaps ink and paper. The 2068's BRIGHT lights a whole cell, so bold text on a colored background makes the background bright too. A bright background on its own can't be shown.

Characters

PicoTerm uses the PC's CP437 character set, the same one PicoVideo uses for 80 columns. BBS menus drawn with box lines, shading blocks and accented letters look as their authors meant.

0632, 64 and 80 columns

Press CAPS+9 to change mode while you work. Choose the mode PicoTerm starts in with Settings, C. Changing mode keeps what's on screen, cut to the new width, and takes up to about a second.

ModeNeedsColorNotes
32any 2068Per characterReadable on any TV. Most BBSes wrap their 80-column screens.
64any 2068One ink and paper for the whole screen; per character with PicoVideoThe 2068's own 64-column mode. Pick the ink in Settings (I); the paper is its complement. Characters are 4 pixels wide, so a sharp display helps.
80PicoVideoPer characterPicoVideo's text mode, on its VGA output. The 2068's own video output shows nothing useful in this mode. Turn on PicoVideo in Settings to use it.

The screen is 24 rows in every mode. Scrolling one line takes about 40 ms in 32 and 80 columns and about 100 ms in 64, which keeps up with text at 2400 baud.

09WiFi

Press BREAK, then W. PicoTerm asks Zimodem to scan (ATW), which takes a few seconds, and lists up to 16 networks. You have to be offline.

  1. Choose a network with the arrows and press ENTER, or press M to type a name that isn't listed (a hidden network). R scans again.
  2. Type the password and press ENTER. PicoTerm sends ATW"name,password" and waits up to 20 seconds.
  3. If it worked, PicoTerm offers to make it the default (Y): it sends AT&W, and Zimodem joins it by itself from then on.
Commas and quotesZimodem's ATW"name,password" can't carry a comma or a " in either part. A network or password containing one can't be joined this way.

picoterm.cfg

S writes everything to picoterm.cfg in the SD card's current folder, and PicoTerm reads it back at start-up. It's a plain text file, so you can also edit it on a PC:

emul=2
cols=1
picovideo=1
ink64=7
bs=8
echo=0
enter=0
blink=1
bell=1
init=ATE1V1X1Q0
phone=Test BBS|bbs.example.com:23|1|2|0|8|0

Emulation: 0 TTY, 1 VT52, 2 ANSI. Columns: 0 is 32, 1 is 64, 2 is 80. bs is 8 or 127. A phone= line holds name, host, telnet, emulation, columns, backspace and echo, separated by |. Lines PicoTerm doesn't understand are skipped, so a mistake can't stop it starting.

11Downloading files

PicoTerm downloads with XMODEM, which nearly every BBS offers. It accepts CRC and 1K blocks, and falls back to plain checksums for older senders. The file goes to the TS-Pico's SD card.

  1. On the BBS, choose the file and XMODEM. The BBS says it's ready to send.
  2. Press BREAK, then X, and type a name for the file on the SD card (download.bin unless you change it). If the file is there already, PicoTerm asks before replacing it.
  3. The transfer starts. The window counts blocks, bytes and errors. Damaged blocks are asked for again by themselves.
  4. At the end, PicoTerm asks Strip ^Z padding? XMODEM pads the last block with ^Z (1Ah) bytes. Say Y for a text file, and N for a program or other binary file, which may really end in 1Ah.
  5. PicoTerm saves the file and shows its size.

BREAK cancels at any time, and PicoTerm tells the BBS. In 64-column mode on a stock 2068, PicoTerm switches to 32 columns for the transfer and back afterwards: it holds the file in the memory the 64-column screen uses.

In this versionThe file is received into memory first, so a download can be at most 6,912 bytes, and a longer one is canceled. It's saved the way SAVE "f:name" CODE saves, so the file on the card starts with a 128-byte +3DOS header. LOAD "f:name" CODE loads it back on the 2068; on a PC, cut off the first 128 bytes. A planned TS-Pico firmware addition should let a later PicoTerm write plain files of any size as they arrive.

13Limits

  • Downloads only, and at most 6,912 bytes in this version. There's no upload, YMODEM or ZMODEM, and no capture-to-file.
  • No scrollback. Text that scrolls off the top is gone.
  • 24 rows. BBSes that assume 25 lines lose one line at the bottom.
  • Color: no bright background on its own; bold makes the cell's paper bright too. 64 columns on a stock 2068 has one color pair.
  • Keyboard: the symbol keys follow the Spectrum's legends, which the 2068 mostly shares. If a symbol on your keyboard sends something else, the table in section 4 is what PicoTerm does.
  • Screen width: PicoTerm can't tell a telnet host your screen width itself. Most BBSes ask, or measure it with the cursor-position question, which PicoTerm answers.
  • The TS-Pico's modem: PicoTerm needs it in Hayes mode, and the card's firmware with the 8251 emulation. It won't work with a stock TS2050.
  • Ctrl-S and Ctrl-Q can't be sent to the host on a call with flow control: Zimodem uses them.

14About this edition

PicoTerm 0.6, written in 2026 for the TS-Pico v3 card, in Z80 assembly (sjasmplus). This manual was written from the program itself. The screenshots come from PicoTerm's own code, run on a model of the 2068 with a simulated modem and BBS, so the BBS screens are examples, not real systems.

Built on:

  • Bo Zimmerman's Zimodem firmware for the ESP32;
  • the TS-Pico firmware and ROM and their Programmer's Manual;
  • the TS 2068 Technical Manual's Video Mode Change Service, the method Zebra's OS-64 uses;
  • the MTERM programs, analyzed for the TS2050 notes.

The CP437 font is the one PicoVideo uses: Bm437_ACM_VGA_8x8 from VileR's Ultimate Oldschool PC Font Pack (int10h.org), CC BY-SA 4.0.

picoterm.tap: BASIC loader (CLEAR 34815: LOAD "" CODE: RANDOMIZE USR 34816), code at 8800h. Ports: 8251 $73/$77, TS-Pico 14/15, AY F5h/F6h, SCLD FFh. Files: picoterm.cfg (text).
